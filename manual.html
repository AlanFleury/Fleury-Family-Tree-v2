Fleury Family Archive

User Manual · Finding relatives, reading family charts, researching evidence, and safely maintaining the family record.

Open the Family Archive

Purpose. This manual explains how to use the online Fleury Family Archive on a computer, tablet, or phone. The archive is intended for approved family members. The names, dates, places, relationships, notes, and sources shown are the information currently recorded; missing details should not be treated as proof that an event did not happen.
Quick start: Open the archive link → sign in with your approved account → use Explore to get started → search for a person → open their details or use Charts to see family connections.

1. Access and signing in

  1. Open https://alanfleury.github.io/Fleury-Family-Tree-v2/. Use this published link rather than opening a downloaded index.html file.
  2. Sign in using the account that the archive administrator approved. Use the same sign-in method and account that you supplied when requesting access.
  3. If access has not yet been approved, use the access-request form. Enter your name, email address, and a short explanation of why you need access, then submit the request. Wait for the administrator to approve the account.
  4. After approval, reload the archive or sign in again. Access is still checked by the server; being able to sign in does not by itself mean the account has been approved for family data.
Staying signed in: The site has been updated to store its authentication cache in the browser to help approved users remain signed in when they reopen the link. This is browser- and device-specific, not a permanent guarantee. Private/incognito windows, clearing site data, browser policies, or expired authentication can require sign-in again. Use the same browser on the same device for the best chance of remaining signed in.

If you cannot get in

  • Check that you are using the correct email/account and that the administrator has approved it.
  • Try refreshing the page. If asked to sign in, complete the sign-in flow rather than repeatedly opening new tabs.
  • If you see “account not linked”, “not approved”, or a request-access page, contact the archive administrator and provide the exact message. Do not send your password.

3. Find a person

  1. Open Explore or People.
  2. Enter a person's name or Person ID in the search field. If several results appear, compare the full name, dates, places, and known relatives before selecting one.
  3. Use filters to narrow the list. Available filters include gender, birth decade, sorting by name/birth/death/ID, evidence status, and whether people have parents, children, or no recorded relationships.
  4. Select the person's name or record link to open their details.
Tip: A Person ID is often the safest way to distinguish people with similar or identical names. Keep the ID when reporting a correction to the administrator.

4. Read a person's record

A person's record may include their name, gender, birth and death dates, birth and death places, notes, sources, parents, children, and spouse/partner links. Some records will have only a few of these fields because the information has not yet been found or verified.

  • Follow a linked parent's, child's, or partner's name to inspect that person's record.
  • Check dates and places against the cited source before treating them as established facts.
  • Distinguish a missing date from an unknown or uncertain date; do not guess a date to fill an empty field.
  • If a relationship appears wrong, note the Person IDs of everyone involved and report the specific link that needs checking.

5. Find relationships

  1. Open Relationships or choose the relationship shortcut in Explore.
  2. Select the first person and the person to compare them with. Use names or IDs to make sure each selection is correct.
  3. Choose Find relationship.
  4. Read the result as a conclusion based on the relationships currently recorded in the archive. If a parent, spouse, or child link is missing or incorrect, the calculated relationship may also be wrong or unavailable.

Genealogical terms such as “first cousin once removed” describe the relationship between two people in different generations. If a result seems unexpected, inspect both people's parent links and compare the family chart before reporting a problem.

Move around and zoom

  • On a computer, drag inside the chart area to pan around the diagram.
  • Use the mouse wheel over the chart to zoom in or out where supported.
  • On a touchscreen, use touch gestures to move around and zoom if supported by the device/browser.
  • If labels are too small, reduce the number of generations, zoom in, or use a wall chart for printing.

Colours and symbols

The chart legend identifies the main visual conventions, including male and female boxes, the highlighted direct bloodline, and marriage/partner connections. Use the legend shown beside the chart rather than relying on colour alone.

Save or print a chart

Use the chart's available actions to print or save as JPEG, PNG, SVG, or PDF. Availability can vary by chart and browser. For a large tree, PDF or SVG is often more suitable than a small raster image. Check the preview before printing.

7. Wall charts and printing

  1. Open Wall Charts.
  2. Choose the starting person and chart style (for example, ancestors, descendants, hourglass, complete family lineage, both parental sides, bloodline, fan, timeline, surname cluster, or family group sheet).
  3. Select the generation depth. More generations usually means a much wider or taller chart and smaller labels.
  4. Choose a paper size from the available options, such as A4 through A0, then create the wall chart.
  5. Inspect the result, then choose Print/PDF or one of the available image/vector export options.

For a chart intended to be read on paper, choose a larger paper size or fewer generations. A printer may split a large chart over multiple pages; check the print preview and page scaling first.

8. Reports and statistics

Reports provides report choices and, where applicable, a person selector and a result limit. Choose the report, supply any requested options, and select Generate report. Use Print report or the available export/save option to keep a copy of the result.

Statistics summarises the data available to the current archive. Statistics describe the current recorded dataset, not necessarily the complete historical family. Incomplete dates, unlinked records, duplicate entries, or uncertain evidence can affect the totals.

9. Places, sources and evidence

Places

Open Places and search for a town, parish, county, country, or other recorded place. If available, choose whether to view all occurrences, birth places, death places, or other places. Select linked people to inspect the underlying record.

Sources & Evidence

Search for a source title, citation text, or person's name. A source entry helps explain where a date, place, or relationship came from. Preserve source wording and citation details when proposing an edit; do not replace a citation with an unsupported conclusion.

Evidence labels

The People Directory may filter records by labels such as Confirmed, Probable, Possible, or Unverified. These labels indicate the current assessment of the evidence, not a guarantee. When adding research, record the source and distinguish a documented fact from an inference.

10. Research tools and exports

  • Compare two people: select two people and compare the details or connections shown by the tool.
  • Family timeline: select a starting person and generation depth, then build a timeline. Results depend on dates recorded in the archive.
  • Relationship matrix: select a person and build the available family matrix.
  • Research list: keep people you want to investigate later. The current interface labels this list as private to the browser, so do not assume it synchronises to other devices.
  • GEDCOM: use the full or selected-branch export options to create a file for compatible genealogy software.
  • CSV/JSON: selected-branch exports are available for supported workflows. Check the chosen starting person before exporting.
Protect exports. GEDCOM, CSV, JSON, reports, and printed charts may contain personal family information. Save them only in a location appropriate for private family records. Do not upload family-data exports or database backups to the public GitHub repository.

11. Editing family records

Editing is intended for authorised users only. If you do not have editing permission, contact the administrator rather than attempting to work around the access controls.

Before making a change

  1. Confirm the correct person using their Person ID and known relatives.
  2. Record the current values and the source supporting the proposed correction.
  3. For substantial changes, ask an administrator to make or review a backup first.
  4. Make one change at a time and verify the result after saving.

Person and family fields

The editor includes fields for name, gender, birth and death information, places, notes, sources, parents, children, and spouse/partner. Use existing people when the correct record already exists. Creating a new record for someone already in the archive can create a duplicate.

  • Parents: select the correct existing parent or create a new parent only when you have checked that no suitable record exists.
  • Children: select the correct existing child or create a new child when needed. Check the option that links a newly added child to the selected spouse/partner as a parent. Leave it checked for children of both parents; untick it for a stepchild or when that partner is not the other parent.
  • Spouse/partner: use the existing record when possible. Recording a partner does not, by itself, change parentage.
  • Dates and places: enter only information supported by your evidence. Keep uncertain or approximate details clearly identified in notes where the editor permits.
Important save/delete safeguard: After using Save person, reopen the record or refresh and verify that the change persisted. If the record does not update, stop and report the issue to the administrator rather than repeating the action several times. Treat Delete person as destructive; do not use it unless the correct record has been confirmed and a backup is available. Deleting a person may affect their recorded relationships.

12. Administrator functions

The Admin section is restricted to approved administrators. The exact controls shown depend on the signed-in role.

  • Pending Access Requests: review new requests and approve only the intended family members. Check the email address carefully.
  • Approved users & roles: assign only the permissions required for that person. Administrator access should be limited to trusted people who need it.
  • Backup: create and securely store a backup before major maintenance, merges, cleanup, or bulk restoration.
  • Restore Master Archive: the administrator can select the original Excel master workbook and restore it through the private authenticated archive workflow. Confirm that the workbook is the correct master before proceeding; restoration can replace or alter archive contents.
  • Gmail access-request notifications: an administrator can connect the designated Gmail account, if configured, to receive access-request notifications and send a test message.
  • Unnamed People Cleanup: scan and preview first. Review each Person ID and relationship count. A scan or preview does not itself delete records. Deleting selected records also deletes their recorded relationships; make a backup and type the required confirmation only after a final review.
  • Merge duplicate person IDs: select the ID to keep and the duplicate ID to merge. Preview first and confirm that the kept ID is the intended permanent ID. Check the resulting person and relationships afterwards.
  • Save/Delete person: verify each change by reopening the record. If a control fails or the result is unclear, stop and investigate before retrying.
Administrator rule: Never perform a bulk restore, merge, cleanup, or deletion without a recent backup and a clear reason. Do not put private family records, backups, or exports into the public source-code repository.

13. Privacy and safe working

  • The published site is the application interface; the private family records are intended to be loaded through the authenticated private API after access is approved.
  • Do not share your password, sign-in codes, or private export files.
  • Be especially careful with information about living people. Record only information appropriate for the archive and its access rules.
  • Sign out or close the browser on shared/public devices. Do not rely on persistent sign-in on a device other people use.
  • Do not commit private family data, Excel masters, GEDCOM files, database snapshots, or JSON backups to the public GitHub repository.

14. Troubleshooting

ProblemWhat to do
Sign-in appears every timeUse the same non-private browser on the same device, allow the browser to retain site data, and avoid clearing cookies/site storage. Sign-in can still be required after expiry or security changes.
Access request / not approvedContact the administrator and ask them to check the exact email address and approval status.
“Account not linked”Send the administrator the exact message and account email. Do not create another account unless instructed.
Person cannot be foundTry the Person ID, shorter name fragments, or alternate spelling. Remove filters that may exclude the person.
Relationship is missing or looks wrongCheck the parents, children, and partner links on both records. Report the relevant IDs and the expected relationship with evidence.
Chart is too large or labels are tinyReduce generations, zoom in, use a more specific chart type, or create a wall chart at a larger paper size.
Save did not seem to workReopen or refresh the record once and check. If the change is absent or uncertain, stop and report it rather than repeating the save/delete operation.
Page appears frozenWait briefly, then refresh if necessary. Reopen the site in a current browser. If the issue repeats, report the page/section, what you clicked, and the message displayed.
Export or print looks wrongTry the browser print preview, landscape orientation, a larger paper size, fewer generations, or a vector/PDF format where available.

15. Quick checklists

For every family member

  • ☐ Sign in with the approved account.
  • ☐ Search by name or Person ID.
  • ☐ Check dates, places, and sources before accepting a claim.
  • ☐ Report corrections with Person IDs and evidence.
  • ☐ Keep exports and information about living people private.

Before an administrator changes data

  • ☐ Confirm the correct person and IDs.
  • ☐ Record the reason and supporting source.
  • ☐ Make a recent backup for substantial or destructive work.
  • ☐ Preview merges, cleanup, and restore operations.
  • ☐ Verify the saved result and linked relationships afterwards.

Manual for the Fleury Family Archive

This guide describes the current application controls and intended workflows. The live interface and available permissions may change as the archive is maintained. If a control is missing, behaves differently, or does not save, follow the troubleshooting guidance and notify the archive administrator.
